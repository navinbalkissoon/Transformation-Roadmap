CRAWL: Foundation

Capabilities Walk depends on. Run in parallel.

C1BAMTech Streaming Platform Control

Deal announced Aug 8, 2017 (Disney already held a 33% stake); 10-K dates the acquisition of a majority interest to Sept 2017
What it is
Disney pays $1.58B for an additional 42% of BAMTech, a streaming technology, data analytics and commerce provider. Later renamed Disney Streaming Services.
Capability created
A proven platform that could power both planned consumer services.
Sources: S1, S2, S5 (pp. on technology platform).

C2Direct-to-Consumer and International Segment

Announced Mar 14, 2018, effective immediately
What it is
New segment combining direct-to-consumer platforms, technology and international operations under one chairman (Kevin Mayer, reporting to the CEO).
Capability created
One accountable owner for streaming platforms, technology and international distribution.
Source: S3. Note: BAMTech later sat inside this segment (S4), but C1 did not need C2 to be complete.

Scales
W2
Full scope
Staged country launches, prioritized by content licenses, willingness to pay, local payments and partner readiness.

Governance decision points the roadmap creates (inputs to the RACI)

D1 · Aug 2017 · Invest in BAMTech control. Disney accelerated an option to take a majority stake; the CEO became BAMTech board chairman and BAMTech's CEO reported to the Chief Strategy Officer (S1). Creates the question: who decides platform investment? (precedes C1)
D2 · Mar 2018 · Combine DTC platforms, technology and international operations. Decision on where streaming technology and distribution authority sit (S3). (C2)
D3 · Oct 2020 · Centralize distribution and ad sales in Media and Entertainment Distribution. One unit decides how content is distributed across streaming and other channels; three content groups produce it (S7). (Run phase, around R1)
D4 · Feb 2023 · Return operational and financial control to segment leaders. Each segment leader gets full operational control and financial responsibility, including technology, sales and distribution; Product and Technology, Advertising Sales and Platform Distribution become shared services (S9). (Run phase, around R2 and R3)

D3 and D4 are linked: D4 largely reverses D3. That reversal is evidence that decision rights for content, distribution and streaming profit were not settled until Run-phase scale exposed the conflict.

Phase rationale

Why Crawl precedes Walk

Crawl holds the two foundations Disney set before launching any consumer service: control of streaming technology and an organization to run it. BAMTech gave Disney a proven streaming and commerce platform instead of a ground-up build, and Disney's filings say it supported the launch of both services. The Direct-to-Consumer and International segment put platforms, technology and international operations under one accountable leader. Remove BAMTech and ESPN+ cannot launch in early 2018; remove the segment and platform, distribution and international decisions stay scattered across businesses. The two run in parallel.

Why Walk precedes Run

Walk tested the platform on two bounded products before any global bet. ESPN+ was one sports service in the United States at a low monthly price; Disney+ launched in the United States first, with other regions staged afterwards. Each produced readiness evidence: ESPN+ passed one million paid subscribers in just over five months, and Disney+ reached 50 million within five months as early rollouts began. Run depends on that evidence because it changes pricing, advertising and app design for a mass audience. Scaling before demand and stability were proven would have put the whole platform at risk.

Why Run comes last

Run scales Disney+ beyond its launch design, and every step changes the organization, not just the technology. International rollout needs local payments, partners and regional leadership; the ad-supported tier reverses the original ad-free model and needs an advertising sales operation; the unified app merges two services onto one platform and one ad-sales effort. These come last because each consumes what Walk proved: a stable platform, a paying base and a launch playbook. The 2020 and 2023 reorganizations show the governance cost: who decides distribution, pricing and profit was contested, and Run depends on settling it.

Known gaps in the public record

  • Disney's initial 33% BAMTech stake (August 2016) is not yet confirmed from a primary source; S1 confirms only that Disney already held 33%.
  • The brief expects Crawl to take 6 to 12 months. Disney's foundation work ran from 2016 to 2018, about two years; this roadmap reflects the case.
  • Public sources say little about Disney's data platform, architecture standards or data ownership. RACI decisions of those types will rest on inference and must be marked as such.
  • The 2019 subscriber target (60 to 90 million Disney+ subscribers by fiscal 2024) may not be comparable to later reported figures, which exclude Disney+ Hotstar. Check before using on the dashboard.
  • No U.S.-only Disney+ launch figure has been located, and the primary Disney release for the August 2025 app unification is still to be found.
  • Oct 2020 and Feb 2023 reorganization details come from Disney releases plus secondary coverage; the 2023 release page is undated.
