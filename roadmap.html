The Walt Disney Company: Streaming Transformation Roadmap

Crawl / Walk / Run reconstruction of Disney's direct-to-consumer transformation, 2017 to 2026, sequenced by dependency.

Member A (Roadmap Sequencer) and Member D (Site Editor): Navin Balkissoon · Member B (Governance Designer): Nelson-Augusto Hernandez Panunzio · Member C (Dashboard Designer): Eferhire Govan

CRAWL: Foundation

Capabilities Walk depends on. Q3 2017 to Q1 2018, about seven months. Run in parallel.

C1BAMTech Streaming Platform ControlQ3 2017

Deal announced Aug 8, 2017 (Disney already held a 33% stake); the 10-K dates the acquisition of a majority interest to Sept 2017
Before
Disney held a 33% minority stake in BAMTech and did not control any streaming platform.
Why
Disney wanted control of the platform behind its planned consumer services.
Decision
Pay $1.58B for an additional 42% of BAMTech, a streaming technology, data analytics and commerce provider, to take a majority stake. Later renamed Disney Streaming Services.
Walk depends on
A proven platform able to power both planned consumer services.
Sources: S1, S2, S5.

C2Direct-to-Consumer Management StructureQ1 2018

Announced Mar 14, 2018, effective immediately
Change
A management structure change: Disney created a Direct-to-Consumer and International segment, combining streaming platforms, technology and international operations under one chairman (Kevin Mayer, reporting to the CEO).
Walk depends on
An organizational structure aligned to the direct-to-consumer strategy, with one accountable owner for streaming platforms, technology and international distribution.
Source: S3. BAMTech later sat inside this segment (S4), but C1 did not need C2 to be complete.

RUN: Scale

Walk initiatives extended to full scale.

R1Disney+ International RolloutQ1 to Q2 2020

Plan (Apr 2019): W. Europe and Asia Pacific in fiscal Q1 to Q2 2020, E. Europe and Latin America from fiscal Q1 2021. Actual: seven W. European markets Mar 24, 2020; India with Hotstar April 2020, during the Covid-19 pandemic
Scales
W2 (stated)
Full scope
Staged country launches, prioritized by content licenses, willingness to pay, local payments and partner readiness.
Org change required
Local partnerships and regional leadership; in India, Disney+ was offered with the existing Hotstar service, which accounted for about 8 million of 50 million subscribers.
Success metric
50 million paid Disney+ subscribers by Apr 8, 2020 (S6), about 8 million of them Hotstar.
← W2 stated S5, S6. Sources: S5, S6, S12 (secondary on the Mar 2020 launch).

R2Ad-Supported Disney+ TierQ4 2022

Announced Aug 10, 2022; launched Dec 8, 2022 (U.S.)
Scales
W2 (inferred)
Full scope
"Disney+ Basic" at $7.99 a month with 100+ advertisers at launch, plus new ad-supported bundles.
Org change required
A global advertising sales operation (later a shared service) serving streaming; a reversal of the original ad-free model.
Success metric
Ad-supported plans reached 37% of U.S. and 30% of global Disney+ subscribers by Q4 fiscal 2024, and about 60% of new subscribers chose them (S15).
← W2 inferred Disney described the tier as a building block toward its subscriber goal (secondary report); S8 does not state it as a dependency. Sources: S5, S8, S9, S15.

R3Unified Disney+ and Hulu AppAnnounced Q3 2025, for 2026

Announced Aug 6, 2025 for 2026; Hulu 100% owned after the Comcast buyout in June 2025 (secondary)
Scales
W2 (stated)
Why Hulu
Hulu carried the general entertainment (FX, ABC primetime) that Disney+, with its family focus, lacked. With Disney owning Hulu outright from June 2025, Disney's stated aims for one app are a better consumer experience and lower churn.
Full scope
One app and one tech platform for branded and general entertainment, news and sports; Hulu replaces the Star brand internationally from fall 2025.
Org change required
Merged product, platform and ad-sales operations.
Success metric
Lower churn after the unified app, Disney's stated aim.
← W2 stated CEO says both services will be "on one tech platform" (secondary: Variety; primary Disney release is a gap). Source: S11.

Governance decision points the roadmap creates (inputs to the RACI)

D1 · Aug 2017 · Invest in BAMTech control. Disney accelerated an option to take a majority stake; the CEO became BAMTech board chairman and BAMTech's CEO reported to the Chief Strategy Officer (S1). Creates the question: who decides platform investment? (precedes C1)
D2 · Mar 2018 · Combine DTC platforms, technology and international operations. Decision on where streaming technology and distribution authority sit (S3). (C2)
D3 · Oct 2020 · Centralize distribution and ad sales in Media and Entertainment Distribution. One unit decides how content is distributed across streaming and other channels; three content groups produce it (S7). (Run phase, around R1)
D4 · Feb 2023 · Return operational and financial control to segment leaders. Each segment leader gets full operational control and financial responsibility, including technology, sales and distribution; Product and Technology, Advertising Sales and Platform Distribution become shared services (S9). (Run phase, around R2 and R3)

D3 and D4 are linked: D4 largely reverses D3. That reversal is evidence that decision rights for content, distribution and streaming profit were not settled until Run-phase scale exposed the conflict.

Phase rationale

Why Crawl precedes Walk

Crawl holds the two foundations Disney set before launching any consumer service: control of streaming technology and an organization to run it. BAMTech gave Disney a proven streaming and commerce platform instead of a ground-up build, and Disney's filings say it supported the launch of both services. The Direct-to-Consumer and International segment put platforms, technology and international operations under one accountable leader. Remove BAMTech and ESPN+ cannot launch in early 2018; remove the segment and platform, distribution and international decisions stay scattered across businesses. The two run in parallel.

Why Walk precedes Run

Walk tested the platform on two bounded products before any global bet. ESPN+ was one sports service in the United States at a low monthly price; Disney+ launched in the United States and five other markets first, with Europe and India staged afterwards. Each produced readiness evidence: ESPN+ passed one million paid subscribers in just over five months, and Disney+ reached 26.5 million paid subscribers within seven weeks across its initial markets. Run depends on that evidence because it changes pricing, advertising and app design for a mass audience. Scaling before demand and stability were proven would have put the whole platform at risk.

Why Run comes last

Run scales Disney+ beyond its launch design, and every step changes the organization, not just the technology. International rollout needs local payments, partners and regional leadership; the ad-supported tier reverses the original ad-free model and needs an advertising sales operation; the unified app merges two services onto one platform and one ad-sales effort. These come last because each consumes what Walk proved: a stable platform, a paying base and a launch playbook. The 2020 and 2023 reorganizations show the governance cost: who decides distribution, pricing and profit was contested, and Run depends on settling it.
